Case 01 Conditional

AI Hiring System — Deployment Decision

A résumé screener auto-rejects 4,400 candidates a year with no human review, across US and EU operations. Deploy, modify, or stop?

Decision
Conditional go on advisory ranking only. Auto-rejection removed. New York City held pending a real bias audit; Ireland held pending vendor documentation.
The call I would defend
I rejected the vendor's bias audit: superseded model version, vendor-commissioned, race and sex reported separately rather than intersectionally. Accepting it was the fast path and would have put a misrepresentation on the client's own website.
The detail most people have wrong
The EU AI Act high-risk deadline moved to 2 December 2027 under the Digital Omnibus. It changed a filing date, not the discrimination risk — Illinois, Colorado, NYC and Title VII were already binding.

Case 02 9 exclusions

ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Statement of Applicability

All 93 Annex A controls for a fully remote health-data SaaS company. Which ones genuinely do not apply, and can you defend that to an auditor?

Decision
84 applicable, 9 excluded with written rationale tied to a factual attribute of the business — each one applicable again the moment that attribute changes.
The call I would defend
I excluded eight physical controls and kept six. Remote working does not delete physical risk; it relocates it into sixty homes the company does not control. Security of assets off-premises matters more here, not less — every asset is permanently off-premises.
Weakest exclusion, flagged as such
A.8.30 outsourced development. It rests on a business decision that could change in a quarter, so it is reviewed at every management review rather than treated as settled.

Case 03 Automated

Privileged Access Review — Spreadsheet to Continuous Control

Forty rows, eleven minutes, everything approved, spreadsheet returned signed. The control produced evidence. Did it produce security?

Decision
A runnable exception engine — orphaned accounts, access after termination, segregation-of-duties conflicts, dormant privileged roles, privileged access with no approval. Managers now decide on 89 records instead of 388.
The call I would defend
The metric I report is not the 77% reduction — it is the retain rate. Above 60% and the automation has not fixed rubber-stamping, it has made it faster. Report the number that can embarrass the programme.

Case 04 Accept w/ conditions

Risk Acceptance — Unsupported Database, 340,000 Records

End-of-support database holding names, dates of birth and bank details. Migration is nine months away. Sign it off or stop the business?

Decision
Accept, with six compensating controls as binding conditions and a hard expiry of 31 March 2027. Miss a condition and the acceptance is void.
The call I would defend
The memo states plainly that "we knew and accepted it" is both the correct way to make this decision and the fact that will appear in litigation. A risk acceptance that only lists benefits is a sales document.

Case 05 In-house rejected

Payroll Vendor Risk, Quantified in Dollars

The CFO asked whether the vendor was worth keeping. The questionnaire came back "amber." Amber is not an input to a budget decision.

Decision
Renew with contract and control remediation. FAIR model, BetaPERT inputs, 100,000-iteration Monte Carlo, every factor documented with its basis.
The call I would defend
Bringing payroll in-house was rejected. It models at $180K against $165K remediated — statistically indistinguishable — for a $600K build and nine months. It moves the risk onto our own balance sheet without reducing it.
Read the distribution, not the average
The mean is what you budget. The P95 is what you survive. A board shown only the average is unprepared for the year that goes wrong.

Method & disclosure

The companies are constructed. The analysis is not. Each case starts from a realistic system pattern, applies regulation and framework requirements current as of September 2026, and ends in a decision a named person could sign.

Every scenario document states that it is a scenario. Nothing here is presented as client work, because a portfolio that dresses up a lab as an engagement loses the room the moment one detail fails to hold.
